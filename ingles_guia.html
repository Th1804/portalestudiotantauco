Unit 3 — Water to your health

Enfermedades que viajan por el agua, cómo se contagian y qué es cierto sobre la ayuda internacional.

Waterborne = transportada por el agua. No es lo mismo que airborne (por el aire) ni foodborne (por la comida), aunque a veces se combinan.

Cholera

Bacteria in dirty water. It causes severe diarrhea and dehydration. People can die if they do not replace water and salts.

Typhoid

Spread by contaminated food or water. A common sign is a high fever. Safe water and handwashing reduce the risk.

Dysentery and diarrhea

Intestinal infections. Dirty water, unwashed hands and food washed with unsafe water are frequent causes.

Hepatitis A

A virus that affects the liver. It can spread when a person swallows something contaminated with fecal matter.

How diseases spread

Fecal-oral route

Germs from feces reach the mouth through water, hands, flies or food. That is the main path of many waterborne diseases.

Everyday situations

  • Drinking water from a river or an open well.
  • Washing vegetables with contaminated water.
  • No latrines, so sewage enters the water source.
  • Floods mix toilets and drinking water.
  • People do not wash their hands after using the toilet.

Prevention

Verbos útiles: boil, filter, chlorinate, wash, cover, build.

Boil water
Hervir el agua mata la mayoría de los gérmenes.
Sanitation
Sistema seguro para eliminar excretas y basura.
Hygiene
Hábitos como lavarse las manos con jabón.
Contaminated
Sucio o infectado; no es seguro usarlo.

Foreign aid: myths and facts

Myth

Foreign aid always ends poverty. Fact: money can help in an emergency, but it does not replace schools, jobs and clean water systems.

Myth

All aid is wasted. Fact: vaccines, oral rehydration salts and protected wells have saved many lives. Some projects fail when they ignore local people.

Myth

Aid is only cash. Fact: it can also be training, technology, medicine or engineers who help build sanitation.

Useful grammar

should / must / can. “People should boil water.” “Communities must have latrines.” “Filters can remove germs.”

Unit 4 — Our way out of poverty

Necesidades básicas, recursos y por qué el desarrollo importa más que una ayuda de una sola vez.

Basic needs

Food, safe water, shelter, health care and education. If one is missing, the others become harder. A sick child often cannot stay in school.

Extreme poverty

People cannot cover the minimum to live. Effects: malnutrition, child labor, illness and leaving school early.

Financial resources

Income (money a family receives), savings, credit and remittances (money sent by a relative who works in another place).

Development

A long process: schools, clinics, roads, fair jobs and public services. Development helps a community meet its needs without depending on emergency aid.

Cause and effect: because, so, therefore. “The well was contaminated, so children got sick.” “They left school because they had to work.”

Compare with care

A village with a clinic is healthier than a village without one. Fewer children miss school when water is safe. More income does not always mean less poverty if food prices rise. Use than, fewer, more and less, and check that the comparison is fair.

Reading

A pump for the school

Last year the river next to the school was the only source of water. After the floods, sewage mixed with it. Many students had diarrhea and missed classes. A local nurse explained that the illness was waterborne: germs from feces had reached the water people drank.

The municipality did not have enough money for a full sanitation system. A foreign organization offered a pump and filters, and the neighborhood added labor. Families learned to wash their hands and to keep the tank covered. The nurse says the aid was useful because people were part of the project. “A machine without training is not a solution,” she said.

Attendance is better now, but the community still needs latrines farther from the well. Clean water was the first step, not the end of development.
